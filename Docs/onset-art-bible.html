ONSETART BIBLE / 01

An art and visual development standard for a new generation of frontier operatives. Built around institutional ambition, readable heroism, expressive equipment and a crisis humanity has not yet named.

MMO Style Action RPGStylised Military Science FantasyInternal Creative CanonVersion 1.0 · 2026
00 / MANDATE

Executive Summary

ONSET is a science fantasy action RPG about earning a place in an elite institution and carrying its standards into an unstable human frontier. The player begins as one among many. Advancement is earned through training, merit, equipment, reputation and service; the player is never framed as a cosmically chosen exception.

Player promise

Join a prestigious academy, choose a role, build a reputation and become a legendary operative through visible, earned progression.

World tension

Human civilisation appears stable while colony losses, missing expeditions and aberration incidents rise. Leadership sees a pattern; the public sees separate events.

Creative mandate

Make every frame legible, aspirational and full of purpose. Military structure gives the world credibility; stylised shape, color and performance give it appeal.

Approved canon: “What is the Academy truly selecting people for?” is a long-range mystery. Keep its answers fragmentary in visual storytelling. The Academy's hidden purpose is unknown even to senior instructors.

Decision hierarchy

1 · ClarityFirst read is immediate.
2 · SilhouetteRole and intent read at distance.
3 · StyleBold, coherent, ownable.
4 · CharacterAppeal and personality.
5 · DetailDetail supports the read.
6 · RealismSelective grounding only.

Audience: concept, character, environment, prop, animation, UX, VFX, technical art, cinematic, marketing, external collaboration, publishing and investment review.

01 / VISUAL IDENTITY

Visual North Star

A bright, heroic academy drama on the edge of a military science fantasy frontier. The image language pairs clean institutional order with vast environments and a low-frequency sense that reality is becoming unreliable.

MOOD BOARD · ACADEMY TO FRONTIER

Composite direction: a confident cadet silhouette in the foreground; monumental academy geometry, distant landscape and one anomalous detail in the depth.

Frame construction

  • Establish a clear subject hierarchy: operative, place, threat, mystery.
  • Use large value groups and silhouette breaks before surface detail.
  • Balance clean human design with one unsettling or unknown element.
  • Use warm light, saturated accents and controlled atmospheric depth.
  • Let architecture and horizon establish scale without burying the hero.

03 / GUARDRAILS

Anti-Inspiration Gallery

These examples describe visual failure modes to steer away from. They are not alternate directions.

GREY ON GREY

Low separation

Armor, terrain and effects collapse into one value family. Increase hue and value separation; restore a readable subject edge.

GRIMDARK EXCESS

Unrelenting bleakness

Constant grime, damage and low-key lighting erase aspiration. Use pressure selectively; preserve moments of color, community and achievement.

ORNAMENT OVER PURPOSE

Fantasy excess

Filigree, runes and ornamental trim obscure military use. Keep technology and psionic expression clean, intentional and function-led.

GENERIC TACTICAL KIT

Anonymous shooter language

Undifferentiated plate carriers, realistic weapon rails and camo reduce class appeal. Establish a distinctive academy equipment doctrine.

DETAIL WITHOUT HIERARCHY

Surface noise

Panels, pouches, greebles and decals compete at every scale. Consolidate detail into a few deliberate groups.

ANIME MMO DEFAULTS

Overfamiliar stylization

Do not drift into generic anime MMO proportions, costume logic or ornamental shorthand. Aim for Arcane, League of Legends and WildStar-inspired stylization without imitation.

Do / Don't reference panel

Do

  • Make every visual element support role, world or narrative.
  • Use a strong large/medium/small shape rhythm.
  • Keep human military design visually hopeful and organized.
  • Let the unknown disrupt a legible baseline.

Don't

  • Use photorealism as a substitute for coherent style.
  • Put grime on every surface by default.
  • Use detail density to imply quality.
  • Make every character, faction and planet share one silhouette.
04 / CREATIVE SYSTEM

Visual Pillars

01 · Readable Heroism

Characters look capable, appealing and ready to act. Their posture, kit and facial expression communicate intent before lore explains it.

Art test: Read the role from a solid black thumbnail and a small gameplay capture.

02 · Earned Progression

Training, service, rank and equipment visibly change the operative. Advancement feels institutional, recognized and earned.

Art test: Can a player tell which gear is a new milestone and why it is valuable?

03 · Structured Humanity

Humanity is coordinated across worlds, but never visually sterile. Academy pride, house culture, relationships and practical field adaptation coexist.

Art test: Does the scene show both the institution and the people living inside it?

04 · Frontier Scale

The worlds are larger and stranger than the academy's maps suggest. Use landscapes, architecture and environmental systems to make exploration matter.

Art test: Does the frame imply a destination beyond the playable vista?

05 · The Wrongness

Aberrations are symptoms across bodies, machines, places and minds. Preserve readability while presenting a broken rule the viewer cannot comfortably explain.

Art test: Does something feel fundamentally wrong before it is labeled a monster?

06 · Expressive Restraint

Stylization comes from intentional shape, timing and color—not visual noise. Details are selected and held to a clear hierarchy.

Art test: Remove one third of the details; does the design become stronger?

Priority stack on every asset: readability → silhouette → style → character appeal → detail → realism. Resolve conflicts from the top down.
05 / STORY IN THE IMAGE

Narrative Themes

Merit vs. Selection

The player earns advancement through visible work, while the Academy's deeper interest remains a question. Do not imply that the protagonist is secretly destined or uniquely preselected.

Public Calm / Private Pattern

Daily life remains stable and functional; leadership-facing spaces may hint at a growing incident map, sealed reports or unusually high recruitment.

Belonging / Rivalry

Houses, cohorts and class identity support competition, friendships, traditions and social life. Rivalry should carry warmth and stakes, not cruelty as the default.

Frontier Responsibility

Human expansion creates obligations. Colonies and independents should read as communities with agency, not disposable mission backdrops.

Power / Discipline

Psionics are real mental influence over reality. They are neither technology, religion nor relic. Training is about intent, control, consequence and focus.

Reality Under Pressure

Aberration events break familiar rules, but escalation must remain legible. Begin with an anomaly, then let patterns accumulate across locations.

“The public sees isolated incidents. Leadership sees a pattern. The Academy is recruiting faster every year.”

Use the quote as a tonal compass, not as fixed in-world copy for every scene.

06 / INSTITUTIONAL DESIGN

Academy Guide

The Academy is one of the most important institutions in human space: an elite training system, social world, military ladder and enduring mystery. It is disciplined without being joyless and prestigious without feeling like a palace detached from service.

Visual inspiration blend: 40% Ender's Battle School · 30% Final Fantasy VIII Garden · 20% Halo Military Academies · 10% Hogwarts (social tradition only)

MOOD BOARD · THE ACADEMY AS A WORLD

Visual direction

  • Large, elegant military campus with civic scale and practical circulation.
  • Clear sightlines, arrival axes, training grounds and visible student movement.
  • Contemporary science-fantasy materials: pale stone/composite, metal, glass, fabric banners and controlled light.
  • House identity appears in sanctioned signage, student spaces and ceremonial moments.
  • Show student life through dormitories, dining, workshops, competition boards and informal gathering places.

Official purpose

Train elite leaders and frontier officers. Make the public-facing mission credible and consistently expressed through training, protocol and service records.

Hidden purpose

Unknown. Even senior instructors know only fragments. Use visual clues with multiple interpretations rather than a definitive symbol or answer.

Student culture

Rankings, houses, rivalries, traditions, friendships and social life are active parts of the academy. Keep lived-in details alongside ceremony.

Production notes

Environment kit

Develop modular wall, column, floor, railing, signage, lighting and threshold families. Reserve a small number of hero pieces for central halls, ceremony spaces and training arenas.

Set dressing

Use authentic student-scale objects—bags, practice gear, projected notices, trophies, repair kits and meals. Avoid generic fantasy bookshelves and unexplained glowing runes.

Do: Make the Academy aspirational, navigable and culturally specific. Don't: Make it a castle, a prison, or a sterile corridor network with house colors pasted on.

08 / CHARACTER DESIGN

Character Guide

Stylised, heroic, readable and attractive, with anime influence kept in expressive shape and performance. Design characters as people within a credible institution, not as unrelated costume collections.

MOOD BOARD · SILHOUETTE & APPEAL

Proportion language

Hands+15%Gesture, grip, role readability

Apply as a starting proportion guide, not a rigid measured formula. Check the full pose and gameplay scale.

Shape and face standards

Silhouette

Use one dominant read, one role-specific secondary read and a small number of signature breaks. Keep face, hands, weapon and class equipment clear in motion.

Face / hair

Expression carries personality. Hair uses large shape masses, strong silhouette and minimal strand detail. Avoid noisy wisps that disappear at distance.

Appeal / variation

Build a broad cast through face shape, posture, body mass, hair, skin tone, age and movement—not only palette swaps. Preserve practical academy and field logic.

Style analysis · Arcane / League of Legends / WildStar

Reference painterly shape control, expressive features, purposeful exaggeration and memorable silhouettes. Do not reproduce identifiable designs. “Anime influenced but not anime” means heightened expression and clean pose language without default anime MMO costume conventions.

10 / PROGRESSION LANGUAGE

Equipment Guide

Equipment is progression and a primary visual reward. Every class originates from the same institutional equipment doctrine: shared undersuits, materials, design philosophy and color systems. Role specialization changes function and silhouette without breaking family resemblance.

Shared grammar

Repeat a small family of fasteners, connector shapes, status lights, seam logic, material transitions, serial marking and academy-issued base components.

Role specialization

Change armor coverage, tool placement, mobility clearance, protection zones and energy interface according to field role. Make function visible without explanatory labels.

Reward hierarchy

Each tier earns a stronger recognition cue: better fit, added field adaptation, rank detail, mission-specific capability, then a historic signature silhouette.

Material family

Technical fabric

Close-fitting, flexible, structured. Matte to satin; never rubbery by default.

Armor composite

Clean edge breaks, deliberate plate coverage, stylised thickness and readable overlap.

Metal

Use for hard connectors, structural edges and weapon interfaces. Keep highlights controlled.

Soft goods

Belts, straps and pads are sparse and functional. No pouch spam or military cosplay clutter.

Family resemblance test: Place four classes together and remove their colors. They should still look issued by the same academy and adapted for different work.

12 / TOOL OF THE ROLE

Weapon Philosophy

Weapons are oversized enough to read and feel like meaningful rewards (+30% proportional guide), but remain ergonomic, wieldable and part of the same world. They communicate combat intent, service level and material culture.

Form follows role

Every silhouette communicates range, handling, firing rhythm and user stance. The shape should remain clear in a ready pose, action pose and gameplay camera.

Institutional lineage

Shared design signatures—grip language, power cells, service seams, sighting logic, status indicators—make the armory feel issued from a common doctrine.

Progression payoff

Upgrades alter recognizable components: barrel or emitter, stock, guard, energy housing, finish, certification marks. Preserve the weapon's family silhouette.

13 / SURVIVAL SYSTEM

Stillsuit Design Guide

Humanity uses a common high-tech survival garment inspired by stillsuit logic: close-fitting, practical, elegant and integrated with life support. It is an everyday frontier layer, not a bulky astronaut suit.

Purpose

  • Environmental regulation and life support.
  • Survival enhancement in hostile climates.
  • Biometric monitoring and field status.
  • Psionic compatibility as a wearer interface—not a psionic source.

Appearance

  • Close-fitting high-tech fabric with clear articulation zones.
  • Subtle tubing and compact interface nodes, placed with a functional rationale.
  • Elegant military tailoring that layers under role armor.
  • Selective vents, seals and surface changes for different climate needs.

Build sheet callouts

Body map

Coverage, articulation, pressure zones, service access.

Life support

Air / thermal interfaces and visible status language.

Biometrics

Small readable indicators; no distracting interface clutter.

Layering

Undersuit remains coherent beneath all class armor.

Avoid: oversized helmets and tanks as the default; glossy rubber full-body suits; exposed tubes without plausible routing; making the suit visually identical to armor.

14 / INTENT MADE VISIBLE

Psionic Guide

Psionics are mental influence over reality. They are real, but not technology, religion or relics. The visual system expresses intent and trained control; energy responds intelligently to the user rather than behaving like decorative electricity.

MOOD BOARD · GUIDED ENERGY

Visual behavior

  • Energy originates in a clear relationship to user intent: gaze, hand, stance or breath.
  • Motion is guided, directed and responsive; avoid random particle spray.
  • Shapes communicate discipline and function before color.
  • Color may vary by discipline; no universal psionic color is canon.
  • Effects preserve character silhouette and enemy read.

15 / ANOMALOUS SYSTEMS

Aberration Design Bible

Aberrations are not a single race. They are symptoms: biological mutation, possession, corruption, altered machinery, psionic distortion or hybrid manifestations. The viewer should think “something is fundamentally wrong,” not “that is simply a monster.”

Design principle

Start with a comprehensible host, object or place. Introduce one broken rule that can be read quickly, then add secondary wrongness on closer inspection. Keep body horror moderate, stylised and readable.

  • Give every aberration a visual origin or symptom family.
  • Distort anatomy, material, timing, reflection, geometry or behavior selectively.
  • Keep the threat's combat silhouette and telegraph separate from its uncanny texture.
  • Use calm space and environmental contrast to make the anomaly feel intentional.
  • Let locations show spread, aftermath or pressure without making every surface corrupted.

Reference gallery · anomaly and altered space

Symptom families

Biological mutation

Recognizable anatomy, altered growth direction, impossible symmetry breaks, limited organic distortion.

Possession / corruption

Behavioral mismatch, compromised identity cues, materials that appear to respond from within.

Altered machinery

Known machine function subtly reinterpreted: movement at the wrong interval, self-repair that violates expected assembly.

Psionic distortion

Space, force or perception bends around a clear focus. Preserve cause-effect legibility.

Hybrid manifestations

Unsettling combinations with a visible shared logic. Avoid random “parts-bin monster” assembly.

Environmental wrongness

Topology, light, sound implication, plant behavior or spatial repetition feels incorrect while remaining navigable.

Reference analysis · Annihilation / The Flood / Control / Warp Corruption

Use ecological uncanniness, spreading biological threat, architectural or perceptual instability and contagious unreality as separate design references. Keep body horror moderate and adapt all inspiration to a stylised, readable combat game.

THREAT SHEETHost baseline / anomaly / combat read / scale variants
ENVIRONMENTAL SPREADEarly / active / severe states with readable safe paths
WRONGNESS DETAILOne central rule break plus restrained secondary cues

Avoid gore escalation, generic tentacle defaults, uniform purple-black recoloring and visual effects that obscure attack telegraphs.

17 / BUILT WORLD

Architecture Guide

Architecture establishes power, purpose and where the player sits within a world. Human construction moves from monumental institutional confidence to adaptable colony systems; alien worlds establish original silhouette languages; aberration zones break environmental rules.

  • Academy: monumental, aspirational, military and grand. Strong axes, human-scale circulation, visible gathering spaces.
  • Colonies: modular, expandable and functional. Show assembly logic, utility, growth and weather adaptation.
  • Alien worlds: distinctive silhouettes and spatial principles rooted in their civilization and ecology.
  • Aberration zones: environmental corruption and distortion. Preserve landmark recognition and playable navigation.

Composition and kit standards

Silhouette

Readable skyline, major masses, landmark spacing, horizon profile.

Scale

People, doors, vehicles, lights and repeated modules establish size.

Material

Material zoning communicates structure, repair, age and access.

Navigation

Landmarks, value contrast, lighting and shape rhythm guide movement.

18 / WORLD CATALOGUE

Planet Catalogue

Each planet type has a visual promise, operational pressure and distinct production needs. A world can host more than one biome, but its dominant visual identity should be clear in a single establishing frame.

ACADEMY WORLD

Academy World

Institutional heartland. Civilised, planned and prestigious; the Academy is a defining landmark rather than a campus hidden inside generic city scenery.

Palette: ivory, blue, gold, landscape green. Story: stable surface, controlled hints of scale and selection.

FRONTIER WORLD

Frontier World

Expansion territories with sparse infrastructure, logistics routes, outposts and unexplored distances. Make the frontier active, inhabited and at risk.

Palette: local earth/sky, human utility accents. Story: the gap between mapped territory and lived reality.

JUNGLE WORLD

Jungle World

Wildlife and exploration. Layered vegetation, clear path landmarks, environmental depth and readable movement corridors.

Palette: rich greens with deliberate warm or cool focal accents. Story: discovery through ecology.

Desert World

Harsh survival and monumental scale. Use Dune-inspired environmental pressure, long horizons, shelter logic and stillsuit relevance.

Palette: sand, mineral shadow, clear sky, controlled human blue/white. Story: endurance and resource discipline.

INDUSTRIAL WORLD

Industrial World

Mining, trade and resource extraction. Industry is a working network of processing, transport, habitation and environmental mitigation.

Palette: industrial neutrals plus safety accents. Story: supply, labor, interdependence and vulnerability.

DEAD WORLD

Dead World

Late game with heavy aberration presence. Strong baseline geography and history make distortion measurable. Preserve navigable silhouettes and threat readability.

Palette: desaturated base disrupted by purple, black and iridescent cyan. Story: escalation and accumulated consequence.

20 / FEEDBACK & SPECTACLE

VFX Guide

Effects reinforce intent, impact, threat and emotion. VFX should read in the first instant, resolve cleanly and return the viewer's attention to characters and play space.

Silhouette first

Maintain a clean contour around the acting character. Place effects at source, path and impact with controlled size and lifetime.

Readable timing

Use anticipation, active event and resolution states. Separate friendly and hostile telegraphs by shape, placement, motion and color.

Material discipline

Use glow, particles, distortion and trails for different jobs. Avoid layering every effect treatment into one event.

Effect design checklist

  • Source is obvious and connected to an actor, weapon, environment or psionic intent.
  • Travel direction and target area are immediately understandable.
  • Impact has weight and recovery; it does not hide the gameplay result.
  • Team, enemy and neutral world effects remain distinct under combat density.
  • Aberration effects suggest unstable rules without obscuring telegraph timing.
  • Scalable settings exist for quality, brightness, particles and screen-space distortion.

21 / PERFORMANCE LANGUAGE

Animation Guide

Animation blend: 40% WildStar · 40% Action Anime · 20% Reality

Combat has readable anticipation, heavy impacts and clear follow-through. Performances are stylised and expressive, but bodies preserve enough weight and recovery to make actions feel consequential. Avoid floaty realism.

Anticipation

Pose and direction communicate action early. Hold long enough for an opponent or player to read it; adjust timing to the attack's speed and game context.

Impact

Use clear contact, compression, recoil, camera response and VFX hierarchy. Heavy actions earn a strong event; small actions stay crisp.

Follow-through

Let weapon, cloth, hair, body and energy finish their motion in a deliberate order. Restore control cleanly and avoid uncontrolled drifting.

22 / PLAYER INFORMATION

UI Guide

The interface is minimal, military and elegant, with Halo and Destiny as broad inspiration points. It prioritizes legibility and player decision-making. Avoid fantasy ornamentation.

Interface principles

  • Clear hierarchy, high contrast, consistent spacing and disciplined typography.
  • Use lines, brackets, compact labels, panels and restrained motion to imply systems.
  • Academy identity is expressed through marks, rank and classification—not decorative filigree.
  • Keep combat information stable and anchored; menus can afford deeper detail.
  • Use motion to confirm state change, not to delay reading.

23 / PUBLIC FACE

Marketing Guide

Major images sell the fantasy and the world in one glance: heroism, progression, scale, discovery and mystery. Key art composes character, environment, threat and mystery as a coherent story frame.

KEY ART COMPOSITION · CHARACTER / WORLD / THREAT / MYSTERY

Key art hierarchy

  1. Character: an operative whose rank, role and appeal read immediately.
  2. Environment: a world with scale, atmosphere and an identifiable location type.
  3. Threat: a readable aberration signal or frontier pressure, not generic monster pileup.
  4. Mystery: one unresolved visual question that invites further attention.

Use asymmetry and depth to direct the eye. Keep title-safe space and campaign crop flexibility. Make the player feel part of a larger institution and a growing response.

Marketing review gates

First read

At thumbnail size, identify the hero, role and main action.

World read

At a second glance, identify academy, frontier, planet or threat context.

Promise

Image communicates earned advancement, discovery, scale or mystery—not a different game genre.

Don't: lean on generic weapon-forward shooter framing, hide the hero in VFX, use unreadable over-rendered backgrounds or imply the player is chosen by destiny.

25 / ENGINE HANDOFF

Unreal Production Standards

Implement the art direction as reusable, scalable systems. The engine should preserve silhouette, color separation, combat information and intentional material response across gameplay distances and performance settings.

Project structure

  • Organize content by feature and asset type: Characters, Environments, Props, Weapons, Materials, VFX, UI, Cinematics.
  • Use consistent prefixes and asset naming. Include faction, archetype, tier, variant and LOD state where appropriate.
  • Keep source art, approved master and engine-ready exports traceable to the same asset ID.
  • Use data assets or tables for shared palette, tier, faction and material parameters where that reduces drift.

Meshes & LOD

  • Validate hero silhouette at gameplay camera distance and expected screen occupancy.
  • Plan LODs around silhouette preservation: head, hand, weapon and role-defining shapes survive.
  • Use nanite or high-detail workflows only where platform, animation and gameplay needs support them.
  • Check collision, sockets, weapon grips, cloth intersections and animation deformation in-engine.

Materials & color

  • Create material instances for shared fabric, composite, metal, emissive and aberrant response.
  • Keep roughness and value separation controlled so forms survive varied lighting.
  • Use emissives as focal accents; expose scalable intensity and avoid bloom swallowing edges.
  • Validate color under target tonemapper, exposure range and accessibility settings.

Lighting & environment

  • Build a consistent key/fill/rim approach per planet mood while protecting gameplay readability.
  • Use fog and post-process for scale and atmosphere; test without post effects as a clarity check.
  • Maintain navigation landmarks and key silhouettes during day/night and weather variants.
  • Audit aberration distortion for motion sickness, clarity and platform cost.

Character and gear implementation

SystemImplementation guidance
Shared bodyDefine skeleton, proportions, sockets, deformation expectations and material zones centrally; keep class variants compatible.
Modular armorUse consistent attachment points, overlap rules and hide/replace masks. Test all supported body types and animation extremes.
WeaponsStandardize grip and muzzle/socket conventions; expose data for animation, VFX origin, sound event and gameplay alignment.
Tier identityUse approved material and mark sets; legendary identity should survive low LOD, silhouette view and grayscale.
StillsuitMaintain a stable base layer; use limited material and color variants without changing the underlying world logic.

VFX, animation and UI implementation

Niagara / VFX

Expose intensity, density, lifetime and screen-space distortion controls. Budget effects by gameplay importance. Validate source, path and impact states.

Animation

Keep authored timing in data or clear animation assets; validate anticipation, hit stop, recovery, root motion and network presentation under actual gameplay latency.

UMG / UI

Use design tokens for spacing, type, color, stroke and state. Support resolution scaling, localization expansion, controller focus, contrast and motion reduction.

In-engine art review: review the asset in its intended gameplay camera, with representative lighting, VFX density, UI overlay, movement and expected LOD. A beauty render alone is not approval.
26 / QUALITY GATE

Asset Approval Checklist

Use this checklist at concept lock, model review, animation/VFX integration and final in-engine review. A single missed high-priority item can invalidate polish elsewhere.

Concept and visual development

  • Canon and section-specific requirements are satisfied.
  • Primary read works at thumbnail and grayscale.
  • Silhouette communicates role, faction or function.
  • Shared ONSET visual language is present.
  • Reference principles are translated into original design.
  • Palette, value range and focal hierarchy are approved.
  • Detail supports form and purpose; clutter is removed.
  • Mood board includes rationale and production target.
  • Front/side/back and scale studies are complete as needed.
  • Do/don't notes and unresolved questions are explicit.

Production and engine

  • Naming, asset ID, source and version are traceable.
  • Topology, UVs, materials, rig or collision meet target standards.
  • LOD preserves signature silhouette and key function.
  • Animation, sockets and attachments are validated in context.
  • Effects preserve attack, ally and threat readability.
  • Lighting and color hold across intended environments.

  • Final in-engine capture is reviewed at gameplay camera.
Approval notes should specify the exact asset version, review context, blocking issues, owner and next review gate. “Looks good” without context is not a production handoff.
27 / NON-NEGOTIABLES

Golden Rules

01

Readability comes first. Make the role, action and threat clear before adding detail.

02

The player begins as one among many. Progress comes from merit, training, equipment, reputation and advancement.

03

The player is not the chosen one. Preserve the mystery of what the Academy is selecting people for.

04

Classes share one institutional equipment doctrine. Specialize by role without making separate visual games.

05

Equipment progression is a core visual reward. Legendary gear is historic, iconic and recognizable.

06

Stillsuits are practical, elegant, close-fitting survival systems—not bulky space suits.

07

Psionics are mental influence over reality. Energy responds to intention; it is not technology, religion or relics.

08

Aberrations are symptoms, not a single race. Make the world feel wrong while keeping threats readable.

09

The Academy is disciplined and social: ranking, houses, rivalry, tradition, friendship and student life belong together.

10

Humanity's stability is real, but the rising frontier crisis is real too. Hold both truths in the image.

11

Use bold, deliberate stylization. Avoid photorealism, grimdark, excessive anime, generic shooter language, clutter and grey-on-grey.

12

Every major frame should carry heroism, progression, scale, discovery or mystery—and ideally several at once.

Make the future look worth defending—and make the frontier feel like it is changing the rules.

This document is the shared visual direction baseline. Any deliberate exception should state what story or gameplay purpose it serves and how it preserves the broader ONSET identity.
